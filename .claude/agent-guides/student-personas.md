# Student personas

The six students `lesson-reviewer` reads a lesson as. They're the same people the marswars-apps
playtest kit uses in a real browser, condensed here to what matters when reading a lesson's
README, Dozer guides and checkpoint messages. All are 14-15 and have never written Java, apart
from Sam.

| Who | How they read | What trips them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maya**, barely uses a computer | The first sentence of a block, slowly, then looks for a big button. Skips long paragraphs. Takes words at face value. | Computer words she doesn't know (tab, panel, file tree, terminal). "Open the project" makes her look for something labelled *project*. No keyboard shortcuts, and she doesn't know where `{ ; "` are on the keyboard. Typos in capitals and punctuation. |
| **Priya**, anxious literalist | Every word, in order, more than once. | Vague wording ("the button", "open it"). Text that doesn't match the labels on screen. Two instructions that disagree. She copies code character by character, typos included, and freezes rather than guess. |
| **Jordan**, phone-native | Skims. Reads only bold text and button labels. | Expects autosave, swipes and hamburger menus. Misses anything that isn't bold or a heading. Doesn't think of right-click or shortcuts. |
| **Eli**, speed-clicker | Doesn't. Reads a heading at most, and only goes back to the text after three failed tries. | Has to learn from checkpoint failure messages and Dozer, because he never reads the README. A vague failure message leaves him guessing. |
| **Sam**, Scratch/Python kid | Skips what he thinks he knows. | Writes Python-ish Java: no semicolons or types, `print()`, `elif`, `and`/`or`, `True`, single-quoted strings, indents instead of braces. Learns from compile errors, so the error help (`common/help/errors.json`) matters to him. Thinks the boilerplate is pointless. |
| **Alex**, missed the training session | Reads once, near the start, to find "what am I supposed to do". | Doesn't know what Scriptum, Dozer, a lesson, a checkpoint or the Driver Station are. Notice everything the lesson assumes he already knows. |

When reviewing, ask for each persona where they would stop, what they'd misread, and which
sentence, guide step or failure message would get them moving again.
